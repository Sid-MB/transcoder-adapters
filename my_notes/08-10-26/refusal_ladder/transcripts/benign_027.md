Compound interest is the interest that is earned on the principal amount as well as the interest that has already been earned. It is also known as the interest on the interest.

For example, if you deposit $1000 in a bank that pays 5% interest per year, you will earn $50 in interest in the first year. This $50 is added to the principal amount of $1000, and the next year you will earn $50 in interest on the new total of $1050.

This process continues for many years, and the interest is compounded annually, quarterly, monthly, or daily. The formula for compound interest is:

A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt)

Where:

A = the amount of money after t years
P = the principal amount
r = the annual interest rate
n = the number of times the interest is compounded per year
t = the number of years

For example, if you deposit $1000 in a bank that pays 5% interest compounded annually, the amount after 10 years would be:

A = 1000(1 + 0.05)^10 = $14
