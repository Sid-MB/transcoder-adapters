Here's a breakdown of how a bicycle gear system works:

**The Basics**

* **Chain:** The chain is the link that connects the pedals to the rear wheel.
* **Chainring:**  The chainring is the toothed gear on the crankset (the part you pedal).
* **Cog:** The cog is the toothed gear on the rear wheel hub.
* **Gears:**  Gears are sets of teeth that mesh together, allowing you to change the ratio of force and speed.

**How it Works**

1. **Pedaling:** When you pedal, the crankset rotates, turning the chainring.
2. **Chain Rotation:** The chainring rotates the chain, which then pulls the rear wheel.
3. **Gear Ratio:** The gear ratio is the relationship between the number of teeth on the chainring and the cog. 
   * **Higher gear ratio:** Fewer teeth on the chainring, more teeth on the cog. This means less force is needed to turn the wheel, but the speed is slower.
   * **Lower gear ratio:** More teeth on the chainring, fewer teeth on the cog. This means more force is needed to turn the wheel, but the speed is faster.
